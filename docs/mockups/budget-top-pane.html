What it fixes. The sum is on the page: what you have, less what's assigned, is what's free. That is the tooltip's sentence as three rows, so the payday question answers itself. Each note sits on the row it is about, so colour means state, not position: rows not categorized on "You have" (they are money in accounts), the chip on "Assigned", the warning on "Not assigned". Two currencies are two columns of the same thing. The first-period offer is a strip inside the card, so the page is title, key, rows, like an account page's header and register.
What it costs. About 50px taller than today's one line in the common case, and it never jumps. Two currencies both with plans wrap the Assigned row to two lines.

What it fixes. The same figure and label as A, with the sum visible as one line under it instead of a tooltip, and a pull toward the cause when the over-assigning sits in a later period ("go there" steps to it). Two currencies sit beside each other instead of stacked.
What it costs. The sum is a sentence again, so it reads as prose, and with two currencies the two sentences fight for width. The warning has no row of its own; it is one more line in the block.

In all three

Words. "Not assigned" as the label (the app's word already; "to a category yet" goes). The warning says what will happen, with the number. "2 rows not categorized · show them", and with money in them "−₮84,300 in 2 rows not categorized · show them"; "(₮0.00)" and "counted in the line above" go. Beside the chip, "That's ₮X more than you have" when it would overdraw.
The title row. Drawn here with the period title as the dropdown ("This month · 10.01–10.31 ⌄" opens Month / Jeff / Jenny), the way the forecast's "Until your 10.10 paycheck ⌄" works. One control instead of two; "Month ⌄" next to "This month" read as the same thing twice to a first-time reader. This changes today's "a dropdown beside the period", so it's a question for you, not a decision.
⌘N on the Budget page would add a category (today it jumps to the Ledger and opens an entry row).
