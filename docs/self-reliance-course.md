# The self-reliance course, as the Savings section's anchor

**Owner's direction (2026-09-19):** Savings gets users onto the first steps of the
Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints course *Personal Finances for
Self-Reliance*. This file records what the course actually teaches, read from the
Church's manual pages (researched 2026-09-19), so the app follows the course and not our
memory of it. Quotes are verbatim. The base URL is
`https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/personal-finances-for-self-reliance/`,
and each page ends in `?lang=eng`.

## The order (chapter 5, "What Is My Current Financial Priority?")

"The first question to which you answer 'no' represents what should be your current
financial priority":

1. "Do I pay tithing?"
2. "Do I have a one-month emergency fund?" (ch. 6)
3. "Do I have health insurance or some other access to medical care?"
4. "Am I free from consumer debt, like credit cards and car loans?" (ch. 7)
5. "Do I have a three- to six-month emergency fund?" (ch. 9)
6. "Am I contributing to a retirement savings fund?" (ch. 11)
7. "Am I working to eliminate mortgage and education loans?" (ch. 10)

This is the strongest anchor for the app. Chapter 12's closing checklist repeats it and
adds "I regularly review and follow my budget" and "I have adequate insurance (such as
health, life, auto, and disability insurance)."

The course frame is the "Financial Stewardship Success Map". Its layers: Pay Tithes and
Offerings → Protect Your Family from Hardship → Eliminate Debt → Save and Invest for the
Future → Continue to Give and to Bless Others. Budget and work are the walls.

## What each step means, in the course's words

- **Tithing first.** "When you receive income, first pay your tithing and then set aside
  money for your future self—even if it's just a little bit. Then, use what is left …
  to pay for your living expenses." Tithing is "one-tenth of our individual increase",
  interpreted as income. It is the first line of the example budget (ch. 3, ch. 4).
- **One-month emergency fund = one month of *expenses*, not pay.** "Your one-month
  emergency fund should equal this amount" (one month's living expenses). Keep it "in a
  safe and accessible place such as a bank account". If it's used, "immediately begin
  putting money back into the fund until it is full." It comes before paying down debt:
  "make only the minimum required debt payment until you have built a one-month
  emergency fund" (ch. 6).
- **Debt.** Inventory each debt with balance, interest rate and monthly payment. The user
  chooses the order: highest interest first or lowest balance first ("they will both lead
  you to be debt free"). When one is paid off, "Roll it over to pay down other loans!"
  The course calls this a "debt elimination plan" (ch. 7–8).
- **Three to six months** comes after consumer debt is gone (ch. 6, 9, 11; ch. 8 says it
  loosely otherwise).
- **Crisis order** (ch. 9): insurance, the emergency fund, then food, shelter, utilities
  and transport first, then call creditors; help from self, family, Church, community.
- **Investing** (ch. 10–11): save, consider home ownership (only once free of consumer
  debt with a 3–6-month fund; mortgage ≤ 25% of monthly income; 1% of home value a year
  for upkeep), education that leads to better work, then retirement (take any employer
  match).
- **Budget** (ch. 4–5): start from monthly *net* income; each line Fixed or Variable; the
  balance column ends at 0; irregular bills divided by the months between them. "Track
  all expenses closely, and adjust your budget categories next month as necessary."
  Review weekly in a family council.
- **Weekly commitments** (each chapter's Commit page), rated red, yellow or green each
  week: track income and expenses every day; build the one-month fund; inventory debt;
  make a debt elimination plan; "continue to put money toward my financial priority".

**Not in the course** (so the app shouldn't claim it): a savings percentage, "savings is
a need", a "debt elimination calendar", a monthly review.
